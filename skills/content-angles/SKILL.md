# Content Angles

A small set of reusable story shapes so you can publish useful content every day without starting from a blank page. Each angle teaches one honest idea, earns trust, and points to a single next step.

## 1. Get the context first

Look for positioning in the current project or any file the user names: an about page, offer or positioning doc, brand notes or past posts. Use what's there for audience, problem, offer and voice.

If audience or offer is still undefined, ask in one message, then go:
1. Who is this post for? (e.g. small-business owners, HR and training leads, teachers, job seekers)
2. What problem or topic is it about?
3. What's the one next step you want them to take? (follow, reply, book a call, download, join a pilot)

Never invent client stories, quotes, statistics or results. If an angle needs a real example (Client, Origin) and none was given, ask for it or write it with a clearly marked `[PLACEHOLDER: real example needed]`.

## 2. The seven angles

Every piece has: **Hook** (first line / first 3 seconds) → **Body** (three beats below) → **CTA** (one next step).

| Angle | Beat 1 | Beat 2 | Beat 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mistake** | The common mistake | Why smart people make it | Why it backfires |
| **Myth** | The belief people hold | Where it comes from | What's actually true |
| **Client** | Their problem and the result they got | What they tried first | Why that didn't work, and what did |
| **Reframe** | A limiting belief the audience has about themselves | What feeds it | Why it isn't true, and the truer frame |
| **Problem** | The problem, named precisely | What's really causing it | What it costs if nothing changes |
| **Prescription** | The pain | Its root cause | One concrete thing to do about it |
| **Origin** | Where you (or your business) were stuck | What was tried | What changed, and the lesson |

Teach the *why* freely; it builds trust. The full *how* can live behind the offer, but every post should still leave the reader with something they can use.

## 2b. The belief thread (stories across four life areas)

Teaching-only, selling-only and proof-only feeds are easy to replace now that AI makes information cheap. What people remember and refer is a person whose point of view they trust. So, alongside the angles, run one **core belief** through four areas of a whole life:

| Area | Covers | Illustrative topic only, not a real story (belief: "more people deserve work that gives them freedom") |
|---|---|---|
| **Work** | career, clients, skills, the offer | Why a short practical course can beat a two-year wait for a credential |
| **Relationships** | family, friends, hobbies | What freedom changed about being present at home |
| **Health** | mind, body, spirit | Burnout taught me what "productive" actually costs |
| **Identity** | roots, culture, age, faith, values | What I watched my parents trade for a paycheck |

How to use it:
1. **Find the belief.** Look for a stated mission or belief in the user's files (about page, offer or brand notes). If none is written down, ask for it in one sentence ("What do you stand for?") and offer to save it to a file for next time. Don't invent one.
2. **One story per area, told from the writer's point of view.** The writer is the main character: their opinion, their approach, what they'd do differently. Pair each with an angle above (Identity → Origin, Health → Reframe, Work → Client or Prescription, Relationships → Mistake).
3. **Close the loop.** Each story should end on the same belief in fresh words, so a reader who sees four posts sees one consistent person.
4. **Choose the depth.** Some stories are safe to tell anyone; some are more personal. The writer decides how much to share. Personal areas (Relationships, Health, Identity) always need a real story from the writer; never fabricate one, and mark `[PLACEHOLDER: your real story]` when missing.

For institutional buyers (schools, agencies, HR teams), keep the ratio roughly 2 work posts to 1 personal one, and tie every personal story back to the work. For someone building a personal brand, the four areas can rotate evenly.

## 3. Hook starters

Adapt; don't paste verbatim every time.
- "The biggest mistake [audience] make when [goal] is…"
- "Stop [common practice]."
- "[Audience] keep asking me about [X]. Here's the honest answer."
- "Why [common approach] isn't getting you [result]."
- "Most [programs/teams] get [topic] backwards."
- "Here's what [N] years of [experience] taught me about [topic]."
- "Are you still [frustrating situation]?"

## 4. Formats

Write in the format asked for; default to a LinkedIn post.
- **LinkedIn post:** 120–220 words, short paragraphs, hook on line 1 under ~12 words, no hashtag walls (0–3), CTA last.
- **Short video script (30–60s):** spoken lines with `[ON SCREEN: …]` cues, hook spoken in the first 3 seconds, ~130 spoken words per minute.
- **Email:** subject (2–6 words, honest about content), 100–200 words, one link or one reply ask.

## 5. Batching a week

When asked for a week or a calendar, rotate through the angles so no two days repeat. Pick a different starting angle each week, and keep one theme per week (e.g. one step of your method or one buyer problem). Output a table (Day · Angle · Hook · CTA), then the full drafts. Note which angle/theme is being tested so results can be compared later. If a core belief is known, make at least one day a belief-thread story from a personal area (section 2b) and add an Area column to the table.

## 6. Quality bar (check before returning)

- Written for one specific reader, not "everyone".
- One idea per piece; one CTA.
- Plain, warm, expert voice. No hype words ("game-changer", "secret", "insane"), no fake scarcity or countdown pressure.
- Every claim is true or sourced; placeholders are marked.
- Institutional buyers are respected: practical, evidence-minded, no guru tone.

## 7. Output

Save drafts to a dated Markdown file the user can open (e.g. `content/YYYY-MM-DD-<theme>.md` in the current project, or the path they name) and show the drafts in the reply. Do not post or schedule anything; publishing is the user's call.
